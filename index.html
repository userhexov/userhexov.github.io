About the Film

Silent Hill is a horror film released in 2006, directed by French filmmaker Christophe Gans. It is based on the iconic survival horror video game of the same name developed by Konami in 1999.

The story unfolds in a fog-covered abandoned town called Silent Hill somewhere in the United States. Ash drifts down from the sky like grey snow, streets are empty, and the silence is wrong in ways that are hard to describe.

What makes this film stand out is how it weaves a story about a mother's love through layers of genuine dread. It is not just about creatures and jump scares — it is about grief, guilt, and things that should have stayed buried.

The film stars Radha Mitchell as the mother Rose, alongside Sean Bean, Jodelle Ferland, and Laurie Holden.

What Is the Film About?

The film opens with Rose Da Silva, a mother desperate to understand why her adopted daughter Sharon sleepwalks and screams the name of a town — Silent Hill — night after night. Against her husband's warnings, Rose takes Sharon there herself.


On the way, their car crashes. Rose wakes up alone. Sharon is gone. The town is blanketed in ash and fog, the streets are empty, and something is deeply, fundamentally wrong with the air itself.

"What makes Silent Hill so unsettling is that you never feel safe. Every time the sirens sound, the world peels back like skin and something worse is underneath."

From personal notes while watching

Rose soon discovers that Silent Hill carries a terrible wound from the past — a tragedy so violent it split the town between realities. When the darkness comes, monsters emerge, and Rose has to survive long enough to find her daughter and understand what this place wants from them.


The ending refuses to give easy answers. It is not a happy resolution and it lingers with you. The kind of film you keep thinking about the next day, turning it over, not quite sure what you saw.
